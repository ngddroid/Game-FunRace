Only what keeps the Game running well

This summary is here to help. The full policy below is what applies.

1About this policy

This Privacy Policy explains how Splash Dash: Obstacle Race 3D (the “Game”), made by NGENDEV TECHNOLABS LLP (NG Studio), Rajkot, Gujarat, India (“we”, “us” or “our”), handles information. It applies to this version of the Game for Android (app ID com.ngstudio.funrace).

The short answer: the Game doesn’t ask who you are, and your save, your display name and your contacts never leave your device. To fix crashes, balance the levels, send notifications and offer updates, it uses a few Google services that work with random IDs, described in section 6. You can switch the game stats, crash reports and notifications off in Settings.

2What’s saved on your device

To remember your game, the Game keeps a small save file in its own private app storage on your device. It contains:

  • Game progressThe levels you’ve cleared, your stars and trophies, and game stats such as races and wins.
  • Virtual itemsYour coins and gems, plus the costumes and other items you’ve unlocked.
  • SettingsYour choices for options such as music, sound effects, haptics and graphics.
  • Display nameThe name you type in for your racer, and the character you pick.

This information is used only to run the Game on your device, for example to pick up where you left off. It is never sent to us or to anyone else. We can’t see it, and we never receive a copy.

Some features, such as daily rewards and challenges, may check your device’s date and time. That check happens on your device, and nothing is sent anywhere.

3Your display name

You can type a display name for your racer. It’s shown only inside the Game on your device. It never leaves your device and isn’t shared with anyone: it’s not part of the game stats or crash reports.

Tip for families

A fun nickname works just as well as a real name. We suggest kids pick a nickname rather than using their full name.

4What we don’t collect

We don’t sell or rent any information, and the Game doesn’t collect information that tells us who you are. In particular, the Game does not:

  • ask you to create an account or sign in;
  • collect your real name, email address, phone number, contacts or payment details;
  • access your location, camera, microphone, photos or files;
  • read your device’s advertising ID (we’ve switched that off), show ads, or use data for advertising or profiling;
  • send your save file or your display name anywhere.

If you email us

If you choose to contact us by email, we’ll receive your email address and whatever you write. We use it only to reply and help you, we don’t share it or use it for marketing, and we delete it once it’s no longer needed for that.

5Permissions

The Game asks Android for these permissions:

  • Vibration VIBRATELets the Game give haptic feedback, like a little buzz when you bump into an obstacle. You can turn haptics off at any time in Settings.
  • Internet INTERNETUsed only for the Google services in section 6: game stats, crash reports, push notifications and update checks. Races themselves work offline.
  • Notifications POST_NOTIFICATIONSLets the Game remind you about daily gifts and share news. On Android 13 and later, Android asks you first. You can turn notifications off in Settings or in Android’s settings.

Vibration and internet are “normal” Android permissions that Android grants when you install the Game. None of these permissions gives the Game access to your information.

The Game doesn’t request location, camera, microphone, contacts or storage permissions.

6Google services we use

The Game shows no ads and has no in-app purchases. It uses these Google services, which work with random IDs rather than your name:

  • Game stats (Google Analytics for Firebase)How the Game is played: levels started and finished, places and times, rewards earned and spent, screens visited, and whether the tutorial was finished. Plus your device model, Android version, language, rough location (country or region), app version and a random app-instance ID. We use this to find levels that are too hard and features that need work. Advertising-ID collection and ad personalisation are switched off.
  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)If the Game crashes or hits a serious error: technical details of the error, your device model, Android version, app version, memory and storage state, what the Game was doing (screen, level, graphics setting) and a random installation ID. We use this only to fix bugs. Google keeps crash reports for 90 days.
  • Push notifications (Firebase Cloud Messaging)A random token that lets us send news to your device, such as new levels or events. Daily-gift reminders are made on your device and send nothing.
  • Updates (Google Play)The Game asks Google Play whether a newer version is available, and Google Play downloads it if you agree.

Google processes this data for us, under Google’s privacy policy and the Firebase terms, and may process it on servers outside your country. We don’t share it with anyone else.

Switch it off

In the Game’s Settings, turn off Usage & crash reports to stop game stats and crash reports, and Notifications to stop reminders and push messages (this also deletes the messaging token).

About app stores

The app store you got the Game from (for example, Google Play) handles your download and updates under its own privacy policy, not this one. Stores may give developers general statistics, such as the number of installs, and, only if you’ve agreed to share usage and diagnostics data with the store, crash and performance reports. These don’t tell us who you are, and we use them only to keep the Game running smoothly.

7Android backup

Android has its own backup feature. If you’ve turned it on, Android may include the Game’s save file in your device backup (for example, a backup stored with your Google account), or copy it when you move to a new device, so your progress can be restored.

This is handled by Android and your backup provider, under your device settings and their privacy policy, not by us. We can’t access these backups. You can manage or turn off backups in your device settings, and a backup copy may remain after you uninstall the Game.

8Keeping and deleting your data

Your save stays on your device for as long as the Game is installed, or until you delete it. You’re in control:

  • Reset progress. “Reset progress” in the Game’s Settings (after you confirm) deletes the Game’s saved data from your device: your levels, coins, gems, unlocked items, statistics and settings. Your display name is kept; change it with “Edit name”.
  • Uninstall. Uninstalling the Game deletes everything it stored on your device.
  • Clear storage. Clearing the Game’s storage in your Android settings does the same.

Your save is never sent to us, so we can’t recover lost progress for you. Android’s own backup (section 7) may be able to restore it, depending on your settings.

Game stats are kept by Google Analytics for the retention period set in our Analytics settings (no more than 14 months), crash reports for 90 days, and an unused messaging token expires on its own. Turning off the switches in Settings stops new data from being sent.

9Children’s privacy

Splash Dash: Obstacle Race 3D is designed for a general audience. We don’t knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13 (or the minimum age set by the law where they live). The Game never asks for names or contact details, doesn’t read the advertising ID, and its game stats and crash reports are tied only to random IDs.

There’s no chat, no online play and no way to share information with other people. The rivals you race against are computer-controlled characters, not real players.

Parents and guardians: if you have questions, or believe your child has sent us personal information (for example, by email), please contact us at store.ngdtechnolab@gmail.com and we’ll delete it promptly.

10Security

Your save lives in the Game’s private app storage, which Android keeps separate from other apps, and we don’t hold a copy that could be leaked. Game stats, crash reports and messaging data travel to Google over encrypted connections.

Using a screen lock and keeping your device’s software up to date helps keep your game data safe too.

11Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live, privacy laws (such as the GDPR in Europe and the UK, or state privacy laws in the United States) may give you rights to access, correct, delete or export your personal data, to object to or restrict how it’s used, and to complain to a data protection authority.

The game stats and crash reports are tied to random IDs, not to your name, so we usually can’t tell which ones are yours. You can stop them at any time with the switches in Settings, delete all of your game data on your device, and contact us with any request; we’ll help as far as we can identify your data.

If you’ve emailed us, you can ask for a copy of that correspondence, or ask us to correct or delete it. We don’t sell or share personal information, and we don’t use it for targeted advertising or profiling.

No surprises

Where the law requires it, we’ll ask for your permission first.
